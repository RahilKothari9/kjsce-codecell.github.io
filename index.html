II.
A small manifesto, printed in full The Thesis

What we mean when we say CodeCell.

A running commentary on why any of this exists in the year MMXXVI.

We are a student society of engineers, designers and the occasional literary type, operating out of a particular fourth-floor room at the K. J. Somaiya College of Engineering in Bombay. We have been in continuous operation since 2012, which by the standards of undergraduate clubs makes us a small and unusually stubborn institution.

The original intent, as best as anyone can reconstruct it, was simple: make a place on campus where students who actually want to write software can find each other without having to apologise for it. That intent has not moved.

A club, if it is any good, is an argument that refuses to end. — working motto, subject to revision

In practice this means we run a handful of things each year — a flagship algorithmic contest, a thirty-six-hour hackathon, a workshop series, and a weekly practice round — and, more quietly, we run a Discord where members argue about editors, ship side projects, review each other's code, and occasionally share an employment lead.

We believe a few unfashionable things. That reading code is at least as useful as writing it. That competitive programming is a tool, not an identity. That the best student clubs behave less like training programmes and more like small editorial collectives — with taste, with opinions, with people who care enough to argue.

If any of the above sounds like the community you were hoping to find, you're already one of us. Recruitment reopens each June — the side door is always unlocked until then.
